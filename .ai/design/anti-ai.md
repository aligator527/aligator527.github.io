# Anti-AI Design Rules

These are hard constraints. Their purpose is not to hide AI usage; it is to prevent generic, statistically averaged interface design.

## Forbidden visual defaults

- Purple/blue/cyan gradient backgrounds without information value.
- Floating gradient blobs, auroras, decorative mesh gradients, or glow clouds.
- Glassmorphism as the primary aesthetic.
- Neon borders, excessive blur, and luminous shadows.
- Dark-first cyberpunk or green-terminal styling.
- Generic star fields, particles, matrix rain, glowing grids, floating spheres, or ornamental WebGL.
- Pseudo-Mac terminal cards with three colored window dots used as decoration.
- Technology-logo clouds.

## Forbidden layout defaults

- Centered `badge → huge headline → paragraph → two pill buttons` heroes.
- Three equal feature cards merely because three columns are easy.
- Bento grids used as a default rather than because information has unequal hierarchy.
- Every section inside a rounded card.
- Repeating the exact same project composition.
- Symmetry everywhere.
- Filling intentional whitespace with decoration.

## Forbidden typography and copy

- Defaulting to Inter/Geist plus rounded cards without deliberate customization.
- Monospace everywhere.
- Typing effects such as `I am a | Developer_`.
- “Crafting digital experiences.”
- “Turning ideas into reality.”
- “Building the future.”
- “Passionate developer.”
- “Code. Create. Innovate.”
- Unsupported superlatives or vague claims.

## Forbidden credibility devices

- Fake testimonials.
- Fake client logos.
- Unverifiable counters such as `50+ projects`, `99% satisfaction`, or `10k users`.
- Invented awards, metrics, responsibilities, outcomes, or technologies.
- Presenting private employer work as open-source work.

## Forbidden interaction defaults

- Applying identical fade-up animation to every element.
- Elastic or bouncing ordinary controls.
- Scroll hijacking or mandatory smooth scrolling.
- Custom cursors that reduce usability.
- Hover interactions with no keyboard or touch equivalent.
- Motion that exists only to make an empty layout feel “premium.”

## Required originality tests

### Substitution test

Replace Ivan’s name with “John Smith,” remove the project-specific diagrams, change the accent color, and ask whether the design still fits any random developer perfectly. If yes, the design is insufficiently specific.

### Content-dependency test

If a project section can be reused unchanged for a completely different project, its composition is not responding to content.

### Screenshot test

At least one viewport crop should remain recognizably this portfolio even without the logo or name because the grid, annotation system, typography, and project treatment form a coherent grammar.

### Purpose test

Every unusual visual or motion element must answer: what hierarchy, relationship, state, or project fact does this clarify?

## Reviewer instruction

Do not call a design “modern,” “clean,” or “polished” as a conclusion. Identify concrete evidence: hierarchy, rhythm, alignment, contrast, responsive decisions, content specificity, and rule compliance.
