# Content Voice

## Voice

Direct, concrete, technically literate, reflective, and internationally readable.

Write like an engineer explaining decisions to another competent professional—not like advertising copy and not like internal corporate documentation.

## Style rules

- Lead with the problem, decision, or result.
- Prefer specific verbs: designed, implemented, migrated, reduced, standardized, coordinated.
- Use short paragraphs and informative headings.
- Explain necessary domain terms on first use.
- Separate facts from interpretation.
- State uncertainty and tradeoffs.
- Use first person for personal decisions and contribution; use “we” only when describing team work.
- Do not erase collaborators by implying individual ownership of team outcomes.

## Case-study posture

Strong case studies show judgment:

- context and constraints;
- alternatives considered;
- why a decision was made;
- how it was implemented;
- what evidence indicates success;
- what would be changed today.

## Avoid

- adjective-heavy self-praise;
- unexplained buzzwords;
- long stack lists in prose;
- fake certainty;
- generic lessons that could accompany any project;
- translating Japanese job titles literally when the English result misrepresents scope.
